## 3. Top 3 recommendations

### Top 1: Xomsteau

- **Pun**: Xomsteau
- **Source**: Gusteau, the fictional chef from Pixar's *Ratatouille*
- **Why it works**:
  - Single token, true "Xom-" prefix — best family fit
  - Pixar's "Anyone can cook" mantra is a free, perfect tagline for a personal home-cooking log
  - Pronounceable on first try (Xom-stoh)
  - Low IP risk — Gusteau is fictional, name itself isn't a registered food trademark
  - Warm tone matches a personal app
- **Tagline**: *Anyone can cook.*
- **Mascot/visual**: chef-hat silhouette, or a stylized rat-with-toque if you want a wink to the source. Soft warm palette.
- **Tradeoffs / risks**:
  - Recognition softer than Chef Boyardee — needs Ratatouille familiarity
  - "-steau" ending faintly French/fancy, could feel mismatched with weeknight chicken
  - Disney/Pixar adjacency creates a faint IP smell even though technically clean

### Top 2: Xom Boyardee (the seed)

- **Pun**: Xom Boyardee
- **Source**: Chef Boyardee (Conagra)
- **Why it works**:
  - Maximum US recognition — universally known
  - "Xom" replaces "Chef" cleanly — structurally elegant title swap
  - Pre-built mustachioed-chef mascot in everyone's head
  - Domain-clear: nobody hears this and thinks music
- **Tagline**: *Whatcha cookin'?* / *Cook it, log it, rate it.*
- **Mascot/visual**: friendly mustachioed chef in toque, warm Italian-grandpa energy. Red/cream palette.
- **Tradeoffs / risks**:
  - Two words — breaks the single-token cadence of Xomify/Xomper/XomFit/XomCloud
  - "Boyardee" is an active Conagra trademark — public launch could draw a C&D
  - Canned-pasta connotation = low-effort food, opposite of "I cook real meals"
  - Reads as an infix/title-swap, not a true "Xom-" prefix word

### Top 3: Xom Fieri

- **Pun**: Xom Fieri
- **Source**: Guy Fieri
- **Why it works**:
  - Unbeatable mascot — frosted tips, sunglasses on the back of the head, flame motif
  - "Flavortown" is a free secondary concept (e.g., "Flavortown Rankings" for top-rated meals)
  - Casual/fun energy matches home-cooking better than fine-dining puns
  - High recognition, fast pronunciation
- **Tagline**: *Welcome to Flavortown.*
- **Mascot/visual**: spiky bleached hair, sunglasses-on-the-back-of-the-head silhouette, flame accents. Loud color palette.
- **Tradeoffs / risks**:
  - Living person — likeness risk if it goes public
  - Two-word name (same family-fit issue as Xom Boyardee)
  - Tonally loud — fights a calm/minimalist UI if you ever want one
  - May date faster than fictional-character puns

---

## 4. Recommendation

**Go with Xomsteau.**

Reasoning:
- Only finalist that is a single-token, true "Xom-" prefix word. Matches Xomify, Xomper, XomFit, XomCloud structurally. Xom Boyardee and Xom Fieri are infix/two-word puns and break the family rhythm.
- "Anyone can cook" is a perfect free tagline for a personal cooking log.
- Lowest IP exposure of the three.
- Mascot direction is flexible — Pixar-cute or classic-French-chef.

This depends on **how strictly you want to enforce the "Xom-" prefix family rule.** Float already breaks it, so there's precedent for flexibility. If the rule is loose, **Xom Boyardee is a strong second** and your seed instinct holds — it has the highest raw recognition.

## 5. Open questions

1. **Prefix rule strictness?** Hard rule or strong preference? Float already breaks it.
2. **Public or personal long-term?** If single-user forever, trademark concerns vanish and Xom Boyardee becomes very tempting. If it might go public, Xomsteau is safer.
3. **Brand voice?** Calm/minimalist (Xomsteau, Xomami, Xomplate) vs loud/maximalist (Xom Fieri, Xom Boyardee)?
4. **Mascot priority?** Mascot-first (Xom Fieri, Xom Boyardee) vs wordmark-first (Xomsteau, Xomami, Xomplate)?
5. **Domain on xomware.com**: any preference between e.g. `xomsteau.xomware.com` vs `boyardee.xomware.com` vs `meals.xomware.com`?

### Phase 3 — Top 3 picks

#### Top 1: Xomfieri

- **Tagline**: *Welcome to Flavortown.*
- **Mascot**: A chibi/cartoon Fieri-stand-in. Spiky platinum-bleached hair, wraparound sunglasses pushed up onto the back of the head, goatee, flame-pattern bowling shirt, holding a flaming spatula. Logo lockup: name in chunky red/yellow flame-cut typeface, mascot's silhouette as the "X." A second-tier "Flavortown" badge is free real estate in-app for top-rated meals.
- **Why it wins**: Highest score on every dimension. Single token now (round 1's "Xom Fieri" was killed by the space; "Xomfieri" reads as one word). Loudest mascot in food, hands down. "Flavortown" is a free in-app concept (rankings, badges, cookbook section). Personal-app status nukes the likeness risk that killed it in round 1.
- **Tradeoffs**:
  - Reads slightly as "Xom-fee-eddy" before you know how to say it (mitigated once you've seen it once).
  - Tonally locks the app into loud forever — if you ever want a calm UI mode, this name fights it. Given the constraints (loud/maximalist), that's a feature not a bug.

#### Top 2: Xomsay

- **Tagline**: *Yes, chef.*
- **Mascot**: Gordon-Ramsay-coded cartoon: spiky blonde hair, deep forehead lines, white chef coat, mid-yell with finger pointed at camera. Steam coming off his head. Logo lockup: name in stark black sans-serif, "X" styled as a crossed pair of chef's knives. A "RAW" / "DONE" verdict stamp UI motif writes itself for meal ratings.
- **Why it works**: Five-letter punch. Maximum verbal aggression in the family. The "Yes, chef" / "RAW!" / "IT'S F***ING RAW!" meme universe is a bottomless content well for empty states, error messages, and toasts. Single-token, perfectly Xom-prefixed.
- **Tradeoffs**:
  - Risks reading "Xom-zay" instead of "Xom-say" on first encounter.
  - Ramsay tone (anger, judgment) is a specific vibe — if the app is supposed to feel encouraging, this fights it. If you want it to roast you for logging frozen pizza, perfect.
  - Slightly less visually unique than Fieri (chef-in-whites is generic; Fieri's silhouette is one-of-one).

#### Top 3: Xomsmash

- **Tagline**: *Smash it. Log it.*
- **Mascot**: An anthropomorphic smash burger — squat, wide, slightly charred edges, holding a metal spatula like a hammer. Sunglasses. Could double as the app icon: a circle = burger patty, the "X" branded into it like a sear mark. Loud red/yellow/black palette.
- **Why it works**: Action verb + on-trend food (smash burgers are peak 2020s loud food). Not tied to a specific celebrity, so it ages better than Fieri/Ramsay. The verb form is functional too — "smash" = "log" in-app vocabulary ("smashed 3 meals today"). Reads cleanly. Mascot is original to you, not a celebrity likeness.
- **Tradeoffs**:
  - Lower instant recognition than Fieri or Boyardee — no celebrity halo. You're building the brand from zero.
  - Slightly burger-coded — domain reads "food" but specifically "burger food," might feel narrow if most of your logged meals are pasta.

### Phase 4 — Recommendation

**Go with Xomfieri.**

It hits every constraint at the top of the scale: single token, Xom- prefixed, loudest mascot in food media, unmistakable visual identity, instant domain clarity. Round 1 only rejected it for being two words and for likeness risk — the new constraints kill both objections (single token now, personal app moots likeness). Flavortown is a free secondary concept layer.

This depends on **whether you want the brand tied to a specific living person.** If yes → Xomfieri. If you want loud-but-original → **Xomsmash** is the clean pick (no celebrity, ages well, mascot is yours). If you want loud-and-judgmental (the app yells at you for logging McDonald's) → **Xomsay**.

**App name:** `Xom Appetite` (English spelling, no accent, two words for display)

**Identifiers:**
- Display name: `Xom Appetite`
- Repo names: `xom-appetite-backend`, `xom-appetite-frontend`, `xom-appetite-infrastructure`
- Subdomain: `xomappetite.xomware.com`
- Lambda prefix: `xom-appetite-*`
- DynamoDB tables: `xom-appetite`, `xom-appetite-ratings`

**Source pun:** *Bon Appétit* — anglicized to dodge the accent.

**Mascot roster (3 chefs, roles TBD):**
- Xom Fieri (Guy Fieri)
- Xom Boyardee (Chef Boyardee)
- Xomdon Ramsay (Gordon Ramsay)

**Family-rule note:** This breaks the strict single-token `Xom-` prefix rule (like Float). Accepted consciously — `Xom Appetite` + `Float` form a "phrase" sub-family alongside the single-token family (Xomify, Xomper, XomFit, XomCloud).
